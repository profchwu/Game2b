# Instructor rubric — AI game-design decision

| Dimension | Strong | Meets | Needs revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learning alignment | Each room teaches a distinct, observable money decision; outcomes and explanations are accurate | Objectives are present but one interaction needs clearer feedback | Theme replaces a measurable learning objective or investment is presented as guaranteed |
| Player fit | One clear action per room, age-appropriate language, no punitive timer, mistakes are recoverable | Mostly clear with minor reading/control issues | Overloaded instructions, frightening pressure, or inaccessible input |
| Evidence quality | AI pre-scores, domain sources, and future player evidence are clearly separated | Some claims lack an evidence label | AI estimates are described as proof of learning or fun |
| Financial safety | Fictional examples teach uncertainty and trusted help; no real products or personalized advice | One phrase needs clarification | Guaranteed-return language, pressure tactics, or actionable investment advice |
| Production feasibility | Scope fits Godot Web and approved Blender style; assets and animation are traceable | Scope is achievable with some cuts | Asset complexity or browser requirements are not accounted for |
| Human decision | Owner explains a choice or revision with a falsifiable test | Choice is stated with limited rationale | AI rank is treated as automatic approval |

## Assessment boundary

The tournament scores in `design-tournament.md` are AI pre-scores. Do not use them as student outcomes. Any classroom pilot needs teacher oversight, age-appropriate consent practices, anonymous observations, and a separate learning measure.
